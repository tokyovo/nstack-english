# Research 2026-11-28
Category: English-learning mobile and web app for absolute beginners whose native language is Vietnamese (pre-A1 to A2; adult everyday, work, and parent-managed kids tracks) | Sources read: 33 (round of 2026-11-27) + 26 (this round: visual design and audio quality), walkthroughs not counted

Framing: users are Vietnamese adults and children with no English. Main jobs: learn first words and phrases with native-language explanations, hear and say them, review so they stick, keep progress across devices, use for free or pay for premium. Features the app has now: three tracks, Vietnamese UI, lessons with listen/choose/type/speak exercises, device text-to-speech (rate 0.9, no slow replay), spaced repetition, sync, child profiles, free/premium entitlements; pictures are typed emoji; a generated-voice slice is planned. This round sets the visual design bar and the audio quality bar (owner's taste in goal.md).

Note on quotes: quotes below are short paraphrases or fragments from the cited page, not copied text. Where a page shows no date, "accessed <date>" is given.

## Walkthroughs
Screenshots in .nstack/research/shots/ (not in git). Observations of patterns only; nothing copied.
- Duolingo (2026-11-28, https://www.duolingo.com/, 390 and 1280 px): steps to first value 10 (home, "get started", pick course, 5 one-per-screen questions with a mascot in a speech bubble, a browser notification prompt, choose "from scratch" or "find my level", then the first exercise); no account asked before the lesson. Exercise types seen: pick the picture card for a word (3 cards, 2-column grid, "new word" tag in purple), matching pairs (10 tiles; at 1280 px each tile shows a number key shortcut). Feedback: selected card fills light blue with a blue border; on Check a full-width bottom sheet slides up, green with a praise line and a short chime (one sound file loaded), or red with the correct answer and a heart lost (5 to 4). Look: white surfaces; one saturated green for primary actions and progress; blue for selection, red for error, purple for tags; illustrations are flat, geometric, 2-3 tones, no outlines, characters with big eyes; cards and buttons have a 2 px light border and a thicker darker bottom edge so they read as pressable 3D tiles; heavy rounded sans for headings. Progress bar at top in green with a lighter highlight stripe. Word audio was not requested in the headless browser (their engineering blog describes generated character voices). Paywall: only a "try 1 week free" banner on the home page. Worked well: picture-first new words, instant colour plus sound feedback. Confusing: matching tiles did not register taps in the headless browser (probably waiting for audio); 9 screens before the first exercise.

## Design bar
The three best-looking products, and why (patterns, not their look):
1. Duolingo: a single saturated brand colour for "go" and progress, a small fixed set of semantic colours (selection blue, error red, tag purple) on white; every new concrete word on a big illustrated card; tactile 3D buttons and cards (a darker bottom lip that collapses on press); answer feedback as a full-width coloured sheet with a chime the instant you check. Apple Design Award, Delight and Fun 2023.
2. Drops: each topic owns a saturated colour used for its tile, header and lesson background, with a simple white pictogram per word; deep base colour with one bright accent for actions; short pleasant sounds. Google Play Best App 2018; design press credits colour coding and illustration.
3. Lingokids: dark warm base with one warm accent, chunky rounded type, tilted outlined cards and mascot cut-outs in colour blobs: playful for kids without visual noise.

Design moves the redesign should match (describe patterns, draw our own art):
1. One saturated colour per track (everyday, work, kids) used for that track's header band, icon tile, progress bar or ring and primary button; neutral surfaces elsewhere; semantic colours for selected, correct and wrong are shared by all tracks.
2. Every concrete word gets a large picture card in one drawn style (flat, 2-3 tones, no outlines, rounded), word under the picture; 2-column grid at 390 px, centred column of about 600 px at 1280 px.
3. Tactile surfaces: buttons and cards with a 3-4 px darker bottom edge that shrinks on press, plus a soft layered shadow for sheets; at least two surface levels on every screen.
4. Instant answer feedback: on check, a full-width bottom sheet tinted green or red slides up with a short sound, a praise or the correct answer (with play button), and the continue button in the same tint; correct choice gives a small bounce.
5. Lesson end celebrates in under 2 s: burst of shapes in the track colour, a big count of words learned, a short rising sound; skippable by tap, reduced motion respected, calmer for adult tracks than kids.
6. Top progress bar in the track colour with a highlight stripe; it moves on every right answer.
7. Onboarding one question per screen with large tappable rows and a small drawn guide character in a speech bubble.
8. Desktop: choices show number-key shortcuts; Enter checks and continues.

One way to beat them for Vietnamese beginners: be Vietnamese-first in the visuals. Set all text in a typeface drawn for Vietnamese (Be Vietnam Pro, SIL OFL 1.1, https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/bevietnampro, checked 2026-11-28) so stacked diacritics stay crisp at heavy heading weights with enough line height; draw pictures from Vietnamese daily life (motorbike, market, noodle soup, school uniform) so a picture is recognised at a glance; put the Vietnamese gloss on the card under the English word; and pair each picture card with a natural voice plus a slow replay (needs 11, 12), which the leaders treat as optional.

Open-licence colour icon sets that fit "drawn colour icons" (checked 2026-11-28 via the GitHub API): Fluent Emoji (https://github.com/microsoft/fluentui-emoji, MIT), Twemoji (https://github.com/jdecked/twemoji, repo MIT; graphics licence per README), Noto Emoji (https://github.com/googlefonts/noto-emoji, OFL 1.1 / Apache per README), OpenMoji (https://github.com/hfg-gmuend/openmoji, CC BY-SA 4.0, ShareAlike). Bundling one set as images (not typed emoji) gives the same picture on every device; verify the exact asset licence before use.

## Not pursued
- Offline lessons: only old sources (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31967249, 2022-07-03; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7392596, 2014-03-13). Device TTS and local progress already make most of it work offline.
- Responsive customer support: operational, not a product feature (ELSA and Duolingo Trustpilot pages above).
- Live tutors / AI conversation partner: changes product scope and cost (learnenglish.life 2026-10; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44934725, 2025-08-17).
- Age-appropriate first words for kids: one source (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id930331514, accessed 2026-11-27, via search snippet); apply as a content rule in the kids track instead.
- Representation of the child's ethnicity in images: one source (same page); recorded human audio and images are out of scope for now.
- Southern vs Northern accent: applies to learning Vietnamese, not English (https://www.clozemaster.com/blog/?p=7799).
- Recorded human voices: goal.md rules them out; generated neural voices with more than one voice meet need 11 (sources above).
- AI conversation partner with voice: changes scope (Trustpilot pages for AI tutor apps, e.g. https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/praktika.ai?page=5, accessed 2026-11-28, report robotic or accented voice glitches).
- Lip-synced talking characters: costly animation pipeline (https://blog.duolingo.com/world-character-visemes, 2022-11-10); a drawn guide character with simple poses is enough.
- Licensed cartoon characters for kids (seen on Lingokids, 2026-11-28): licensing cost, and the goal requires original or open content.
